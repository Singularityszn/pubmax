# Migrations and row-level security

Rules whose subject is SQL: the migration labels, the RLS waves and the permission matrix. The captain applies migrations; agents ship SQL only.

Repo-wide laws and the index of every other area file are in the root [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).

## Migrations and policies

- **WE KEEP THE PRICES, AND WE REMEMBER THESE ACCOUNTS AND WHAT THEY LOGGED.** Captain 5 Sep 2026, over verification scout verify-preview-4 sections 7.3, 10 and 13: a throwaway account deleted itself through the product, its two Pint Drops stayed on the map exactly as they should, and both were still printed under the retired handle, one of them leading the Blackfriar sheet on production. Migration `0150` + rollback is BOTH halves and neither is optional. (1) THE LEDGER REMEMBERS: `public.account_retention_ledger` is one row per departure, holding the auth user id, the retired handle, the day it left and the ids of everything it logged in seven lanes, written by the tombstone trigger FIRST, before any row of that account is deleted, because the Memories, the Moments and the wall photo rows are gone by the end of the same statement. Client roles get NOTHING (revoked grants plus a deny policy), because the table pairs an account with its public contributions and that pairing is exactly what the deletion took away from every reader. (2) THE NAME COMES OFF AND NOTHING ELSE DOES: `lib/retiredContributor.ts` is the whole reading rule and a PURE LEAF, spending `ANON_HANDLE_LABEL` rather than inventing a second word for one idea; the three lanes that PRINT a contributor handle to a stranger carry `author_retired_at` (`pint_drops`, `structured_visit_reports`, `weather_recommendations`) and each swaps it in its ONE public projection (`toDTO` and `enrich`, `toVisitReportDTO`, `published`). A retirement is a NAMING change: the price, the measure, the date and the AUTHORITY KEY are untouched, so a lone drop stays lone and a confirmed pair stays confirmed, and no lane predicate in `lib/venues.ts`, `lib/pintTrust.ts` or `lib/priceTier.ts` may ever read the stamp. A moderator still sees the handle, exactly as on an anonymous drop. `community_prices` is in the ledger and takes no stamp, because its `contributor_handle` reaches a reader only through `public_contributor_leaderboard`, which `0150` narrows with one `tombstoned_at is null` predicate; `pint_drop_comments` is deliberately absent, its handle being typed by the commenter beside an IP hash and proving no account. A stamp rather than a join, because the answer is a fact about the row and a public read may not pay for it, and handles are RESERVED by the tombstone so a retired one is never re-issued. The permalink lane names its columns rather than selecting `*`, so it asks for the stamp and falls back to the pre-`0150` list on a 42703. Pins: `__tests__/retiredContributor.test.ts`, `__tests__/accountRetentionLedgerMigrationEffective.test.ts` (real PostgreSQL 16 under the `0145` storage guard replica: the fault reproduced before, the ledger, the stamps, the board and the client refusal after, then the rollback).

- **RLS is a second line of defence; the service role is still the write path.** App stores use `requireSupabaseAdmin` and bypass RLS. Migrations `0065`-`0069` (`supabase/migrations/2026080320*_rls_wave2_*.sql`) add JWT policies derived from API ownership plus explicit client-deny on capability and secret tables; helpers live as `public.rls_*`. Hard rules the policies must keep: `visit_reports` SELECT matches `canViewOnPublicSurface` via `rls_can_read_visit_report`, so hidden and pending rows are never PostgREST-readable; `community_prices` SELECT is `hidden_at is null` only, with no actor exception and no `hidden_at` column grant; `private_account_identities` is owner SELECT only. Do not re-open `using (true)` on private tables; do not grant `plan_crew_members.token_hash` or `community_prices.actor` to browser roles. Rollback: `supabase/migrations/rollback/20260803200000_rls_wave2_rollback.sql`. Captain applies migrations; agents ship SQL only. Effective proof is `npm run test:rls`, which requires rollback to reproduce the pre-wave catalog byte for byte. SQL shape pins: `__tests__/rlsWave2Policies.test.ts`.

- **THE PERMISSION MATRIX IS PROVED AT BOTH DOORS, AND A HELPER LIVES WHERE THE POLICIES READ IT.** `docs/security/PERMISSION_MATRIX.md` is the expected table over a private Plan, a Social Crew, a private conversation and its attachment, a private Moment and its object, a photo tag, a save, a price and its confirmation, moderator actions, deletion and the export; `__tests__/permissionMatrixEffective.test.ts` (in `npm run test:rls`) asserts every cell at the route AND at the table over ALL migrations, with the bearer seam as the one double and the real `ADMIN_TOKEN` gate. SEVEN ROLES, one cluster, one matrix: a second authorization matrix would be two answers to one question, so widen this one. The roles are owner, invited participant, unrelated user, BLOCKED user (a mutual follower the owner has blocked, so a refusal measures the block and not a missing follow), guest (a device RSVP: a Plan seat with a capability token and NO account, which reads the Plan it is a seat on and names nobody at the inbox, the Wanted list, the export or deletion), removed member and staff. EVERY DENIED CELL IS HELD TO THREE RULES: the honest status, no disclosure asserted over the WHOLE serialized body rather than the fields a reader remembered to check, and no side effect asserted as a truth read either side of the attempt; an id that names nothing must answer exactly as one somebody else owns, or the refusal is an existence oracle. `rls_enabled_no_policy` on a service-mediated table is NOT a finding to silence with a policy (Astra F07, 6 Sep 2026): the route holds the service-role key and RLS never runs for it, so the route door is where that advisory is answered. THREE THINGS THE WIDER RUN WRITES DOWN, all of them the code being right and a first-pass assertion being wrong. THE LINKED HANDLE WINS: `resolveMessageHandle` prefers the handle the caller's account owns, so a signed-in caller naming somebody else's handle at the inbox or the saved-pub door acts on their OWN list rather than being refused, and only an anonymous caller asserting a CLAIMED handle is 403. `conversations` and `messages` are no longer deny-all, whatever 0019's own comment still says: 0066 granted SELECT to `authenticated` behind two participant policies, and SELECT is the whole grant. And an owner policy FILTERS a statement rather than erroring it, so a stranger's write succeeds against zero rows and a table cell must assert that nothing MOVED, never that the statement failed. Its first run found the `0144` hole: `0070` moved every RLS helper into `pubmax_private`, so `create or replace function public.rls_is_plan_participant` in `0124` wrote a copy no policy reads, and a removed member kept table SELECT on the Plan until `0144`. Redefine a moved helper in `pubmax_private`, never in `public`, and never set `ADMIN_TOKEN` unset in a route test that means to prove a moderator cell, because the gate opens for everybody under `NODE_ENV=test`. A keyless cluster has no GoTrue, so the deletion seam deletes the auth user row itself and the `0078` trigger is what is proved.

- **A MIGRATION'S FOUR-DIGIT LABEL IS THE NUMBER THE CAPTAIN APPLIES BY, SO IT NAMES ONE FILE.** Apply ORDER is the 14-digit timestamp (`scripts/qa/migration-apply-list.mjs`, pinned by `__tests__/migrationApplyList.test.ts`), but the label is what an apply instruction says out loud, so two files carrying one label is an ambiguous instruction rather than a naming nit: #1500 and #1501 merged within an hour and both called themselves 0142, which is why the messages unread index is 0143. Take the NEXT free label off `ls supabase/migrations` before naming a file, and rename the later file when two land together. `__tests__/migrationVersions.test.ts` is the fence: it refuses a duplicate label in `supabase/migrations` AND in `supabase/migrations/rollback/`, names the offending pair in the failure, and refuses a rollback whose label no migration carries. `GRANDFATHERED_DUPLICATE_LABEL_FILES` is the closed list of collisions that predate the fence and may only ever SHRINK. A MIGRATION ALSO LANDS WITH THE WAY BACK OUT, and from label `0092` that is a rule the same fence holds: 0120 and 0121 shipped without one, so a social-connection lifecycle and a Wanted promotion could be applied and not undone, and `MIGRATIONS_WITHOUT_ROLLBACK` is the exception list, empty today and shrink-only, with a second case failing a row that already has a twin. 89 older migrations predate the practice and sit below the floor. A rollback says what it COSTS rather than what it restores, and it is proved rather than reasoned about: `docs/proof/migration-rollback-twins/` applies every migration after the v1 baseline to a throwaway PostgreSQL 16 cluster through `scripts/rls/session-harness.mjs` and reads the catalog either side. THE OTHER HALF IS THE SCHEMA A HELPER IS REDEFINED IN: after `0070` moved the `rls_*` helpers into `pubmax_private`, a `create or replace function public.rls_...` writes a SECOND function no policy reads, so the migration applies cleanly and the security answer does not move (`0124`, fixed by `0144`); `__tests__/rlsHelperSchema.test.ts` refuses one in any forward migration dated after the move, a rollback restoring the public copy being legitimate and a DROP being the remedy, with `0124` the sole grandfathered entry.

- **EVERY MESSAGING CHANNEL IS PRIVATE, AND ONE SEND IS ONE MESSAGE.** Adversarial review of the 5 Sep 2026 merge day, F-1 (P0). A Supabase PUBLIC channel authorises on the API key alone, and the anon key is in every browser, so the payload-free signal above went out on public topics: handles are public and enumerable (`GET /api/profiles/directory`), so one websocket on `live:inbox:<victim>` was a live activity oracle on a named person, and because both participants' inbox topics ride ONE batched send, two handles pinging together are in one conversation - subscribing to N handles reconstructed the private DM graph without reading a message body. The payload was never the leak; the metadata was. FOUR rules. (1) `private: true` ON BOTH HALVES OR NEITHER: `lib/messagesBroadcast.server.ts` sends it and `lib/messagesRealtime.ts` subscribes with `{ config: { private: true } }`, which makes supabase-js send the browser's own JWT; a channel opened public on one side hears nothing from the other. (2) THE POLICY IS THE GATE AND IT LIVES WHERE THE POLICIES READ IT: migration `0148` + rollback adds `pubmax_private.rls_may_read_messaging_topic(text)` and ONE permissive SELECT policy on `realtime.messages`, admitting `live:inbox:<handle>` to the account that owns the handle (through `rls_owns_handle`, so an alias still works) and `live:messages:<uuid>` to a participant (`rls_is_conversation_participant`), and answering false for everything else. The helper takes the topic as a PARAMETER rather than calling `realtime.topic()`, so it is creatable and provable on a cluster with no realtime schema; `scripts/rls/session-fixture.sql` now carries the `realtime.messages` + `realtime.topic()` shim the platform provides, which is what lets every all-migration suite apply 0148. The migration RAISES rather than skipping when that table is absent: a security policy that quietly did not install is worse than one that refuses to. The server broadcasts with the SECRET key, which bypasses RLS. (3) A DROPPED CHANNEL IS RE-ASKED, not abandoned: one transient `CHANNEL_ERROR` used to downgrade a thread to polling for the life of the mount, which is the ordinary case on a waking phone; `messagesReattachDelayMs` is a bounded backoff capped at `MESSAGES_REATTACH_MAX_ATTEMPTS`, and a join that LANDED clears the budget. (4) ONE SEND IS ONE MESSAGE: the composer latches on a ref claimed BEFORE the first await (React state is committed a microtask later, so two taps in one task both posted), mints one `clientMessageId` per attempt, and migration `0149` + rollback makes the second arrival a conflict the store answers with the row it already wrote; a missing column answers with an unkeyed retry rather than a lost message. A REFUSED send keeps the drinker's text, prepended to whatever they have since typed, and never overwrites a newly picked attachment. Pins: `__tests__/messagesBroadcast.test.ts`, `__tests__/messagesRealtime.test.ts`, `__tests__/messageSendIdempotency.test.ts`, `__tests__/messageThreadRequests.test.tsx`, `__tests__/messagingRealtimeAuthorizationMigrationEffective.test.ts`, `__tests__/messageIdempotencyMigrationEffective.test.ts`.

- **A PINT DROP'S REAL HANDLE NEVER LEAVES THROUGH THE TABLE.** Fable full-repo review B-2. `0066` granted authenticated SELECT on the Pint Drop table (renamed `pint_drops` by `0118`) behind a row policy that still admits anonymous rows, and `0067` did the same for `structured_visit_reports`. The grant is the whole row: the anonymous author's handle, moderator notes, report reasons, receipt keys, and a retired handle beside its stamp. `community_prices` listed `contributor_handle` in its column grant. The table stayed in the `supabase_realtime` publication, so a postgres_changes subscription delivered that raw row. Migration `0171` + rollback revokes those two SELECTs, revokes the compatibility view `visit_reports` with them, drops `contributor_handle` from the column grant, and takes `pint_drops` out of the publication. The live nudge is a payload-free PRIVATE broadcast on `live:pint-drops`, the same shape as `0148`: `lib/pintDropsBroadcast.server.ts` sends it after the row is stored and `lib/realtime.ts` subscribes with private set. The policy `pubmax_pint_drops_topic_read` on `realtime.messages` admits that one topic to authenticated and nobody else; a browser with no session keeps the poll. The payload stays empty, and the caller refetches through the filtered route. The rollback puts the grants and the publication back, which republishes the anonymous handle to every signed-in account, so it is a disclosure and the header says so. Pins: `__tests__/pintDropsTableDoorMigrationEffective.test.ts`, `__tests__/pintDropsBroadcast.test.ts`, `__tests__/realtime.test.ts`, and the table cells in `__tests__/permissionMatrixEffective.test.ts`.

- **A PRICE'S BILL IS A COLUMN, AND IT IS NULLABLE ON PURPOSE.** Migration `0153` + rollback adds `public.pint_drops.receipt_photo_key`, the Storage key for the photo of the bill every NEW price now carries (captain 7 Sept 2026; the rule itself is `lib/pintDropReceipt.ts` and it reads no column). THREE rules. (1) ADDITIVE AND NULLABLE, because the requirement is on a new price at the WRITE DOOR and never a retro-judgement of a row already on file: every drop written before 0153 has no receipt, and no read may treat that as a fault. (2) The app's additive-rollout guard (`isMissingReceiptColumnError`) saves the drop WITHOUT its key until the captain applies this, so a deploy that runs ahead loses a photo rather than a price. (3) A THIRD SLOT, not a reuse: `pint_photo_key` is what they drank and `venue_photo_key` is where, and only this one is evidence for the FIGURE. The rollback drops the column and every key in it, leaving the objects unreferenced in Storage, so sweep those first if the bytes must go too. Pins: `__tests__/pintDropsStore.test.ts`, `__tests__/pintDrops.test.ts`.

- **WHO CAN SEE A PROFILE IS A COLUMN, AND THE ROLLBACK IS A DISCLOSURE.** Migration `0154` + rollback adds `public.profiles.visibility`, the account-privacy wave's one visible choice (the rule itself is `lib/accountVisibility.ts` and `lib/AGENTS.md` owns it). FIVE rules. (1) ADDITIVE, NOT NULL, DEFAULT `public`, so the default STATES what every existing row already was rather than changing what anybody agreed to, and a constant default rewrites no table on PostgreSQL 11 or later. (2) THE CHECK MIRRORS THE CLOSED WORD SET, the `lib/drinks.ts` idiom, so a third answer is a migration; `__tests__/accountVisibility.test.ts` derives the CHECK's own text from `ACCOUNT_VISIBILITIES` so the two cannot drift. (3) NO GRANT AND NO POLICY MOVES IN 0154: the column inherits `profiles`' RLS. Migration `0170` revokes table writes from `anon` and `authenticated`, keeps SELECT for `authenticated` behind `profiles_owner_select`, leaves full DML with `service_role`, and drops `profiles_owner_insert` and `profiles_owner_update`. 0067 granted that SELECT and kept those write policies; it never revoked the table grants, which is why a signed-in JWT could rewrite its own row. Widening a grant on the visibility column would hand a browser the one column that says what to withhold, and it would say it about every account at once. (4) A DEPLOY AHEAD OF THE APPLY SERVES EVERY ACCOUNT AS IT DOES NOW, an absent column reading as public, but it CANNOT SAVE a choice, and the store's guard REFUSES that write rather than answering 200 to somebody who asked to be private. That is the opposite of `0153`'s receipt-photo guard on purpose: losing a photo is a cost, losing a privacy choice is a lie. (5) THE ROLLBACK IS NOT NEUTRAL AND ITS HEADER SAYS SO: dropping the column republishes every private account's bio, city, favourite drink, interests, workplace and linked socials, and nothing in the database remembers that anybody asked otherwise, so the application comes back first or that disclosure is the cost. Pin: `__tests__/accountVisibilityMigrationEffective.test.ts` (real PostgreSQL 16: the fault before, the default, both words, the refused third, the null, the unmoved grants and policies, then the rollback), plus the four private-card cells in `__tests__/permissionMatrixEffective.test.ts`.

- **OWNING A HANDLE HAS TWO AUTHORITIES, AND A MISSING STAMP IS NOT AN ABSENT OWNER.** 0148 admits both messaging channels through ONE question, `pubmax_private.rls_owns_handle`, and that question rested on ONE column, `public.profiles.user_id`. Production carries a NULL there on 22 of 30 live profiles (verify-preview-5 section 17.1), so the policy refused those handles both channels and the surfaces fell to their poll with nothing on screen. THE COUNT IS NOT A POPULATION OF LOCKED-OUT ACCOUNTS, and measuring that first is what stopped the obvious fix being the wrong one: a profile row is minted two ways, and only one of them knows an account. `claim_pubmaxx_handle` (`0097`) INSERTS the row with `user_id` already set, so a claimed handle is stamped from its first instant; `profileStore.ensure()` inserts a HANDLE ALONE and is called by the one-tap price, follow and check-in paths, so a handle that has only ever been written ABOUT gets a row with no owner. Those rows can never be claimed afterwards, by design: the RPC answers `taken` and `lib/profileOwnership.ts` refuses to let an account inherit an unowned row. Measured on production, all 10 auth users are accounted for (8 hold a stamped profile, 2 have claimed no handle at all), so the 22 have no owner to find. Migration `0152` + rollback is the hardening the measurement still justifies, because ownership resting on one column is fragile in a way data cannot show until it bites: `profiles_user_fk` is ON DELETE SET NULL, so the column is CLEARED by a deletion. FOUR rules. (1) THE SECOND AUTHORITY IS `public.private_social_accounts` (`0071`), the other place this product writes down who owns a profile, and its branch fires only when the row carries no `user_id` of its own, the identity store binds THIS `auth.uid()` to THAT profile, the caller owns no other profile (`profiles_user_id_unique` says the same thing, the predicate says it first) and the profile is not tombstoned. Nothing reads handle TEXT as evidence, which is what F-1 was about. (2) `rls_is_conversation_participant` IS NOT REDEFINED: it already delegates both handle branches to `rls_owns_handle` and inherits the answer whole, so one place decides what owning a handle means and the 0144 drift cannot start. (3) THE BACKFILL TOUCHES ZERO ROWS ON PRODUCTION AND THAT IS EXPECTED: `0092`'s `provision_social_product_account` REQUIRES `profiles.user_id` before it will create a social account, so every existing `private_social_accounts` row already points at a stamped profile. It is there for the rows a future path, a restore or another environment can produce. (4) THE CLAIM PATH REPAIRS ITS OWN STAMP: `claim_pubmaxx_handle` used to find the caller by `user_id` alone, so an account whose stamp had been cleared was told its OWN handle was `taken`; it now asks the same second authority and re-claims idempotently. The rollback keeps every stamped value, because un-stamping a correct owner to undo a true statement costs more than it returns. SEPARATELY, 15.3's single thread refusal is NOT a policy defect: the predicate was measured admitting three live linked participants on production as `authenticated`, so a refusal there is a join that raced the conversation row, and the client then spends `MESSAGES_REATTACH_MAX_ATTEMPTS` and settles on the poll. Pin: `__tests__/messagingChannelOwnershipMigrationEffective.test.ts` (real PostgreSQL 16 under the `realtime.messages` shim: the fault reproduced before, the owner admitted and every stranger, departed account, unowned handle and anon caller refused after, then the rollback).

- **SCOUT HYGIENE IS THREE MOVES, AND `pub_presence` IS NOT ON THE DEFAULT-DENY LIST.** Migration `0159` + rollback (scout report section 1b): (1) DROP `round_spends_round_client_ref_idx` because `0057`'s `unique (round_id, client_ref)` already backs the same key (Supabase linter 0009). (2) `0068`'s `pub_presence_client_deny` was `FOR ALL`, which is also permissive on SELECT and overlapped `0007`'s `pub_presence_public_read`; replace it with INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE deny policies plus ONE SELECT rule (`expires_at > now()`). Writes stay service-mediated; the permission-matrix exception lives in `docs/security/PERMISSION_MATRIX.md`, not the 0068 closed list. (3) Covering indexes on hot unindexed foreign keys and `pub_presence (expires_at, created_at desc)`; unused-index linter notices are intentionally ignored. Pin: `__tests__/supabaseHygieneMigrationEffective.test.ts`.
