<a id="a-camera-move-has-an-owner-and-the-map-a-reader-is-looking-at-loads-first"></a>

- **A camera move has an OWNER, and the map a reader is looking at loads first.** The canvas takes ONE `focusPoint` fed by TWO owners, the opening-location answer and the area lane (everything through `moveMapCameraTo`), so the token is an IDENTITY carrying its owner (`lib/mapCameraFocus.ts`), never a bare counter each owner starts at 1. A viewport that names nowhere may not be read from: while the opening question is open the map's placeholder bounds are the WHOLE WORLD, so `openingLoadViewportFor` answers the city's own view, and the ring around a settled viewport waits for idle while the viewport keeps the caller's turn (`VIEWPORT_SHARD_RING` / `NEIGHBOUR_SHARD_RING`). Captain's law: only that map loads, then the sides fill in. The `/map` ceiling in `perf/route-budgets.json` comes DOWN at the next CI sweep, never up. A restored `q` frames the matching venues and keeps the venue sheet closed, including after a reload. Only an explicit `sel` restores a venue sheet. Typed search keeps its single-result selection behavior. Search matches own camera framing while surrounding price pins stay visible. Pins ignore the query but retain every other active filter, whether the search field is focused or closed. A typed search that selects or frames matches releases the held Near me membership. Selecting a venue also releases that membership, including either search-suggestion path. An explicit selection keeps camera ownership, so a deferred search cannot replace it. Route fitting includes all road coordinates as well as stops and reserves space for measured controls. In short landscape, an active route hides the ambient city-status stack while retaining route status and its last-train action. The header and route actions still reserve measured space. A road upgrade or resize refits only while the route still owns the camera. A user gesture or explicit search releases that ownership. The automatic arrival turn preserves it. Explicit route recovery uses `route-recovery` and can restore ownership after a gesture ends. The [user guide](../../README.md#features) describes the recovery control. Pins: `__tests__/mapWalkingRouteAdoption.test.tsx`, `__tests__/mapQueryCamera.test.tsx`, `e2e/map-short-walk-framing.spec.ts`. Pins: `__tests__/mapCameraFocus.test.ts`, `__tests__/mapViewportFirstShards.test.ts`, `__tests__/mapArrival.test.ts`, `__tests__/mapQueryCamera.test.tsx`, `e2e/map-restored-price-pins.spec.ts`.

  Search uses `mapSearchNameTier` in `lib/mapSearchIndex.ts` for loaded and indexed venue names, including aliases. Whole-name exact matches lead. Name prefixes and word prefixes share the next tier, followed by name substrings. Area discovery applies only when no name tier matches. An area score never replaces a matching name's tier. In Pints mode, equal relevance puts pubs with supported pint prices before unpriced pubs, then other venue kinds. Known current-city venues supply the price evidence for every representation, including indexed area matches and name matches beyond the loaded-row limit. Their current prices override compact snapshots before the index limit and the final merge. Unloaded venues retain only the evidence available in the compact index. The compact index applies this order before its result limit. `mergeMapSearchVenueSuggestions` in `lib/mapSearchSuggest.ts` applies the same relevance and pint order to the current city's combined group. Other drink modes preserve loaded-result order, with exact indexed names promoted ahead of weaker matches. Display and keyboard navigation use the same combined sequence. Regression coverage lives in `__tests__/mapSearchIndex.test.ts`, `__tests__/mapSearchSuggest.test.ts`, and `__tests__/mapSearchCurrentCityOrder.test.tsx`.

<a id="nothing-moves-this-camera-but-the-reader-and-there-is-one-compass"></a>

- **Nothing moves this camera but the reader, and there is ONE compass.** Captain 3 Sep 2026: the idle ambient orbit is DELETED, not switched off, because it turned the map at 0.6 degrees a second whenever a reader stopped touching it. While a gesture is on the glass no camera intent runs at all, and for `GESTURE_CAMERA_HOLD_MS` after it only the REACTIVE intents (`route`, `query`, and the arrival turn) stay out, never a move the reader asked for: `lib/mapGestureGuard.ts` is the rule and `scheduleCamera` in `components/map/canvas/useMapCamera.ts` is the one place it is asked. MapLibre's compass is off at the source, because its reset flattened the pitch to a view no city opens on; `lib/mapCompass.ts` owns the one control, which eases BOTH axes back to the city's designed attitude. It lives in the Layers popover from 7 Sep 2026 rather than on the map edge: what that rule refuses is a compass that appears and vanishes with the map's own attitude, and a control the reader opens one button to reach is not that. A phone has no Layers popover, so the same action is `Reset view` in the Layers tab of `More map controls`, handed up by the canvas through `onCameraResetChange`; it is always rendered and disabled while the camera is on the city's attitude. `components/map/canvas/cameraProbe.ts` publishes the camera to the browser suite the way `components/map/canvas/paintedPinProbe.ts` publishes the pins. Pins: `__tests__/mapGestureGuard.test.ts`, `__tests__/mapCompass.test.ts`, `e2e/map-gestures.spec.ts`.

<a id="a-pin-s-colour-may-be-a-hint-a-pin-s-figure-may-not"></a>

- **A pin's COLOUR may be a hint; a pin's FIGURE may not.** Pins print the price itself from `PIN_PRICE_LABEL_MIN_ZOOM` (`components/map/canvas/filters.ts`, which owns the gate and the `text-field` expressions; the tag's size, offset and padding are at the top of `components/map/canvas/buildScene.ts`). The default pint band (`priceBucket`) may be fed by a demo seed so an unpriced city pack still reads as a map; its label is a claim, so `formatPinPriceLabel` in `components/map/canvas/geojson.ts` reads a strictly narrower stack (contributor price, curated `cheapestPrice`, favourite-pint price, never `latestDemoPrice` and never a `priceBand` without a price) and only for prices admitted by the [venue price contract](lib-venues-areas-listings-and-nights.md#a-venue-kind-is-a-closed-list-with-one-owner-and-a-non-pub-kind-is-present-but-n) through `hasPintPriceSemantics`. A selected non-pint lens replaces both band and label from its corroborated category price, names the drink in the label, and makes missing prices neutral. A famous bar, late-food or restaurant venue's default `cheapestPrice` is its anchor price, which printed bare would masquerade as a pint. An uncorroborated report cannot reach either lane. The tag rides `pubs-point` itself and takes the ordinary collision deal (`text-optional`, no `*-allow-overlap`), so where it will not fit the LABEL goes and the pin stays. Pins: `__tests__/mapSymbolCollision.test.ts`, `__tests__/canvas-geojson.test.ts`.

<a id="a-pin-s-fill-says-the-price-its-edge-is-what-makes-it-findable-and-dark-mode-nee"></a>

- **A pin's fill says the price; its EDGE is what makes it findable, and dark mode needs two tones for that.** Dark roads are subordinate warm context in `lib/mapBasemapTaste.ts`, but night ground, water, buildings and road tiers still span distinct tones, so no single rim edges every price band everywhere. `venuePinEdgeTokens` (`components/map/canvas/tokens.ts`) therefore publishes a rim plus an opposite-luminance casing in dark and nothing in light, and `makeVenuePinDraw` in `lib/mapIcons.ts` draws the casing pass first at `GLASS_CASING_STROKE` so the rim can drop to a hairline and the band keeps its area. Do not reach for a brighter band instead: `--brick` is a muted rose because dark `--brass` is a coral, and this map's rings are already spoken for. The UK base pub takes the SAME edge, for the same reason. `__tests__/mapPinBandContrast.test.ts` reads the shipped dark tokens rather than a restated copy and holds every band to an edge over every dark basemap tone.

<a id="a-cluster-is-a-neutral-disc-that-reads-from-its-cheapest-price-and-a-priced-pin-"></a>

- **A CLUSTER IS A NEUTRAL DISC THAT READS "FROM" ITS CHEAPEST PRICE, AND A PRICED PIN IS A PILL FROM STREET ZOOM.** Owner decision 16 Oct 2026 (supersedes the band ring below): a cluster printed its cheapest price but wore the ring of its commonest band, so "£2.49" stood in the red "dear" ring. Now the disc is paper with an ink casing and a NEUTRAL `--line` ring that carries no band, "from" sits over the cheapest figure, the figure wears the ink of ITS OWN pub's band (`--price-band-*-ink`), and "n pubs" hangs under the disc; the spoken label is "from £x · n pubs" (`clusterLabelText`, `lib/donutClusterGeometry.ts`). The band reaches the GL label through `minPriceKey` (pence × 10 + band, reduced by supercluster as a `min`, `CLUSTER_PRICE_KEY_PROPERTY` in `components/map/canvas/filters.ts`), so the cheapest figure and its band are one claim; a disc with no price prints its count in ink. The map key says the same (`lib/mapPriceLegend.ts`). Pins: `__tests__/mapClusterHonesty.test.ts`. Captain's design lane, 7 Oct 2026, as it stood before that: A cluster used to be a disc FILLED by its commonest price band, which painted every dear pocket of London in the coral that owns the Plan CTA (`--brick` shares the coral family, DESIGN.md One Accent Rule) and answered "how many pubs" when the map's job is "where is the cheap pint". Now (1) the disc is `--panel-raised` with a 1.25px ink casing, the band is a 3px RING and never a fill, and the figure in it is the cheapest price any pub inside says, with the count as a small number on the rim from ten pubs up; it is one of two sizes by zoom (44px, 36px from z13) and never grows with its count. (2) The figure is a claim, so it reads `clusterPrice`, which `components/map/canvas/geojson.ts` stamps from exactly the sayable price the pin's own tag prints and from nothing wider (no lens figure, no Spoons round, no estimate, no demo seed, no anchor price), reduced by supercluster as `minPrice`; `CLUSTER_FIGURE_EXPR` in `components/map/canvas/filters.ts` writes it the way `formatPinPriceLabel` does and `__tests__/mapClusterFigure.test.ts` holds the two to one answer. A cluster with no price prints its count. (3) The desktop DOM donut and the GL layers (`clusters-casing`, `clusters`, `cluster-count`, `cluster-count-badge`) are one design in two renderers, sized from `lib/donutClusterGeometry.ts`. (4) From `PIN_PRICE_LABEL_MIN_ZOOM` a priced pub's mark is a PILL (`pubs-price-pill`, a nine-slice image stretched to its figure by `icon-text-fit`, band as a 3px foot) layered ABOVE `pubs-point`, so it places first and the glass and its tag, which sit on the same point, collide with it and drop; where the pill finds no room the glass places as it always did, so a pub is never lost to make its price fit. A pub wearing a mark (Pint Drops ring, what's-on ring, band halo, provisional or confirmed dot) or the selected pub is NOT a pill, because each of those rides on or round the 28px glass and a pill would bury it. There is no tail: a pill is centred on its pub, so the probe, the hit box and the camera keep one answer to "where is this pub". The selected pub's tag is the one inverted figure (paper on ink). (5) Landmark pictograms are information, not a mark to find a pint by: 22px from the overview to z14 and 36px only in the inspector band, with the River ring. The GL figure is Noto Sans Bold because the basemap's glyph server has no JetBrains Mono, so it is tabular but not the data face; the DOM donut uses `--font-data`. Contrast: `__tests__/mapClusterContrast.test.ts` holds the casing-or-paper edge to 3:1 over every basemap tone in both themes and the pill's hairline likewise, and records that amber on paper is under 3:1 in light, which the casing and the figure cover. Pins: `__tests__/mapSymbolCollision.test.ts`, `__tests__/donutClusterGeometry.test.ts`, `__tests__/canvas-geojson.test.ts`, `__tests__/mapIcons.test.ts`.

<a id="an-aging-price-looks-aging-never-fresh"></a>

- **AN AGING PRICE LOOKS AGING, NEVER FRESH.** Owner decision 16 Oct 2026. A pin's printed figure is judged on the window its Price Standing owns (`priceAgeFor`, `lib/priceTier.ts`): a drinker's log is fresh for 30 days, a published price for 365, and a figure with no read day is never fresh. `scripts/build_slim_index.mjs` stamps each pub's `priceObservedAt` (the latest read of a row carrying its cheapest figure, `lib/priceReadDay.mjs`), `components/map/canvas/geojson.ts` stamps `aging` on a pin whose own base figure is past its window (`pinPriceAging`; a lens, Spoons or favourite-pint figure is not judged), and the pin draws its glass with a BROKEN RIM (`venuePinIconKey(kind, band, true)`, `AGING_RIM_DASH` in `lib/mapIcons.ts`). The colour is still the band and nothing else. An aging pub is never a price pill, because a pill prints the figure on a clean edge. The venue sheet breaks the plaque's edge (`priceBadge--aging`) and says why in words (`priceAgeNote`). The map key carries the mark. Pins: `__tests__/priceAge.test.ts`, `__tests__/pinPriceAging.test.ts`, `__tests__/pintTrust.test.ts`.

<a id="the-map-draws-its-icons-at-build-time-and-a-phone-draws-only-what-the-sprite-can"></a>

- **THE MAP DRAWS ITS ICONS AT BUILD TIME, AND A PHONE DRAWS ONLY WHAT THE SPRITE CANNOT ANSWER FOR.** Captain report 19 Oct 2026: the map is very slow on an iPhone. The scene drew all 79 designed icons (landmarks, TfL marks, 56 glasses, 4 pills, 2 base marks) on EVERY style load, one canvas, its paths and one `getImageData` read-back each. Measured on a production build, iPhone 14 emulation at 4x CPU, interleaved A/B, five pairs per theme: that drawing held the main thread for a median 207 ms in light and 260 ms in dark, inside the gap from `pubmax:map-style-load` to `pubmax:map-icons-ready`. Registering the pre-drawn sprite instead takes 19 ms and 15 ms. Style load to icons ready fell from 766 to 531 ms (light) and from 829 to 495 ms (dark); style load to scene built from 953 to 801 ms and from 1,156 to 643 ms; style load to `pins-visible` from 5,259 to 4,444 ms and from 5,261 to 4,504 ms. The cost is one request on `/map` (114 to 115 median, ceiling 160) and 60.5 KB (light) or 68.7 KB (dark). FIVE rules. (1) NOTHING IS RETYPED: `npm run build:map-icon-sprites` (`scripts/build-map-icon-sprites.mjs`) runs the REAL draw functions (`lib/mapIcons.ts`) with the REAL colour recipe (`iconTokensFor` and `readTokens` in `components/map/canvas/tokens.ts`) over the REAL stylesheets in Chromium, and encodes the PNG from the bytes the canvas read back. Captured in the running app, all 79 icons the old path handed MapLibre are byte-identical to the sprite in both themes, and MapLibre renders the two with 0 differing bytes. (2) ONE REQUEST, NO CANVAS: `components/map/canvas/iconSprite.ts` fetches only the open theme's atlas, from map construction, and inflates it with the browser's own `DecompressionStream`, so there is no read-back and no colour management. Each row is filter type None, which is also the smallest encoding here (60.5 KB against 75.4 KB adaptive). The decoded icons stay for the life of the page, so a theme swap back costs a copy. (3) THE DRAWING STAYS AS THE FALLBACK, NEVER A HOLE: the scene draws, exactly as before, when the atlas has not arrived, failed, cannot be decoded, lacks a spec, or was drawn in colours the live theme does not have (`iconTokensMatch`, which allows a browser 2 of 255 for rounding a computed `color-mix()`; the legacy legibility mode changes `--ink` and so draws). It is all or nothing per style. The missing-image resolver gives one of our ids its real icon, never the transparent pixel a basemap miss gets. (4) THE MAP'S PIXEL RATIO NEVER TOUCHES THEM: the images are added at an explicit `pixelRatio` of 2, so a map that starts at 1 and calls `setPixelRatio(2)` keeps every image, fetches nothing and keeps 2x data. MapLibre's `@2x` URL rule belongs to its style-sprite loader, which this path does not use, and five namespaces through that loader would cost ten requests per theme. (5) A CHANGED INPUT REBUILDS THE SPRITE IN THE SAME COMMIT: `scripts/lib/mapIconSpriteFingerprint.mjs` hashes the artwork, the colour recipe and the CSS custom properties behind the icon colours (comments and formatting excluded), and the fence fails until the sprite is redrawn. Pins: `__tests__/mapIconSprite.test.ts`, `__tests__/publicAssetCaching.test.ts`.

<a id="a-map-that-never-mounts-is-the-shell-s-problem-and-nothing-on-map-may-wait-for-i"></a>

- **A MAP THAT NEVER MOUNTS IS THE SHELL'S PROBLEM, AND NOTHING ON /map MAY WAIT FOR IT FOR EVER.** F08/J33. `components/PubMapCanvas.tsx` diagnoses every way a MOUNTED map dies and each has its own watchdog and honest heading; none can fire when the canvas never mounts, and MapLibre lives in its own chunk behind a dynamic import on purpose. Measured with that one chunk aborted: `/map`, `/map?sel=` and `/map?log=1` all answered with `app/error.tsx`, so a blocked map library took the venue list, the selected pub's sheet and the Pint Drop composer with it. `lib/mapCanvasAvailability.ts` is the whole of the shell's policy and it answers TWO things the canvas cannot: `module` (a `MapCanvasBoundary` in `components/PubMap.tsx` caught the subtree) and `timeout` (`MAP_CANVAS_READINESS_CEILING_MS` lapsed). FOUR rules. (1) The ceiling runs from mount until the canvas reports MapLibre construction, readiness, or its own failure. Construction transfers timeout ownership to the canvas's first-frame, scene-ready, and pin-reveal watchdogs. The loading frame cannot stop the ceiling because shell-owned pins can release that frame without a canvas. (2) RETRY BUILDS A FRESH LAZY: React caches a rejected one for ever, so `pubMapCanvasForAttempt` keys one per attempt and remounting the same component would replay the rejection without touching the network. (3) There is ONE venue view, `components/map/MapFallbackCard.tsx`, shared by the canvas's five failure kinds and the shell's two, and ambient banners stand down while it is up because a road-closure note describes a map the reader cannot see. (4) A CANVAS IS THE ONLY THING THAT REPORTS MAP BOUNDS, and three arrival lanes (search arrival, UK place arrival, national browse) wait for that report before reading a shard: with no canvas `/map?sel=<id>` sat at ZERO venues for the life of the page while a bare `/map` loaded 141, which took the card's rows, the list and the log intent with it, so the shell hands the loader the opening view once per attempt through that same door. Two phone geometry defects rode with it: the fallback card reserved the chrome from `--mobile-map-rail-h`, a property defined NOWHERE, so the `calc()` was invalid at computed-value time and the card's heading sat behind the top bar (it reads `--mobile-map-chrome-full-h` now, published by `components/mobile/mobileMapShell.css`), and the basemap Retry cleared the tab bar alone, so `elementFromPoint` at its own centre answered the plan-activation bar and the tap was swallowed; it reads that stack member's own published berth and height instead. Pins: `__tests__/mapCanvasAvailability.test.ts`, `__tests__/mapSurfaceChrome.test.ts`, `e2e/map-blocked-fallback.spec.ts`; proof `docs/proof/map-blocked-fallback/`.

<a id="the-pins-arrive-during-the-arrival-flight-never-before-the-basemap"></a>

- **THE PINS ARRIVE DURING THE ARRIVAL FLIGHT, NEVER BEFORE THE BASEMAP.** Owner decision 15 Oct 2026: on `/map` the price pins show while the opening camera is still flying, not after it lands, and the flight stays. Measured on a location-granted open, where the flight zooms 12 to 16: the reveal gate armed at scene build, forgot the basemap tiles that style had already painted, and MapLibre's default `cancelPendingTileRequestsWhileZooming` then dropped the hold view's tiles still in flight, so the basemap had no frame and the pins (rightly) waited until the flight had landed and its tiles answered. TWO rules, both in `components/map/canvas/pinRevealCoordinator.ts`. (1) `basemapPaintCarriesIntoReveal`: a primary basemap tile THIS style generation already painted counts toward the reveal it arms; one from a replaced style, from before a context loss, or erased by a silent tile retry does not. (2) `openingCancelsPendingTiles`: a newly built map keeps its in-flight tiles while it zooms, so the hold view paints (scaled) mid-flight, and its first `idle` hands every later zoom back to MapLibre's default. The basemap gate itself is unchanged: no pin paints before a basemap tile has, and pins are drawn by the same camera in the same frame, so they cannot sit at a stale position. REDUCED MOTION KEEPS ITS GATE: its camera jumps, there is no flight to overlap, and both rules answer the old behaviour for it. Pin: `__tests__/pinRevealCoordinator.test.ts`.

<a id="the-opening-prices-stand-on-a-picture-of-the-opening-streets-and-only-for-the-ca"></a>

- **THE OPENING PRICES STAND ON A PICTURE OF THE OPENING STREETS, AND ONLY FOR THE CAMERA IT WAS TAKEN FROM.** Captain report, 19 Oct 2026: the phone map is very slow. On production in iPhone 14 emulation at 4x CPU the document's discs arrived at 4.2 s and stood on blank paper, and the basemap did not show until the engine's reveal. Under the discs, `components/map/MapOpeningPreview.tsx` now lays a PICTURE of the opening London basemap, captured by `npm run build:map-opening-snapshot` (`scripts/build_map_opening_snapshot.mjs`) and committed as `public/landing/map-opening/london-light.webp` and `public/landing/map-opening/london-dark.webp` with `lib/mapOpeningSnapshot.json` beside it. FIVE rules. (1) IT IS THE BASEMAP AND NOTHING ELSE: the OpenFreeMap style the map opens on (`MAP_STYLES`) with the app's palette applied by the canvas's own `applyBasemapTaste` and `readTokens`, served to a bare MapLibre page from the repo's TypeScript; no pin, cluster, landmark, rail line or chrome, because a picture of a pin is a price claim the picture cannot keep current. (2) IT IS FLAT, AND THE BROWSER TILTS IT: captured straight down (pitch 0, bearing 0) at the opening zoom over the ground `openingGroundRect` says a 440 x 960 phone can see at the opening pitch, plus 24 px, and laid on the discs' own CSS plane, so every street lands where the engine paints it at any phone size. A picture captured already tilted matches one viewport height only, because the engine's camera stands 1.5 viewport heights away. Its labels lie on the ground where the engine stands its labels up; the streets, the river and the canal coincide. (3) IT SITS UNDER THE STAGE: a fixed layer at z-index 0 before the shell, so the stage's chrome, the loading line and the discs stay on top. While it shows (`html[data-map-preview="on"]`, `:has(.mapOpeningGround)`, max 440 x 960), the skeleton and the loading frame are see-through and the canvas is held at `opacity: 0`, so a half-drawn engine frame never covers it; the loading line's pint glass holds still over it, as it does under reduced motion, because its pour repaints every frame and over see-through paper each one drew the picture again (the map's own pins about 0.7 s later, median of six cold runs at 390x844). That is why the preview's handoff after `pubmax:pins-visible` waits for the loading frame to lift (rule above). A larger viewport, a theme not in use and a visit the preview is not on fetch nothing, because a background image under `display: none` is never requested. (4) A STALE PICTURE IS NEVER SHOWN: `resolveOpeningGround` answers null unless the snapshot's camera is `MAP_OPENING_PREVIEW_CAMERA`, so another city, a resumed view, a deep link or a moved camera opens as before; and `__tests__/mapOpeningSnapshot.test.ts` fails when the camera, the styles, the covered viewport or the image hashes no longer match, until the script runs again against a production server of the tree. Re-run it also when the basemap palette changes (`lib/mapBasemapTaste.ts`, the map tokens), which no test can see. (5) IT PAYS ITS WIRE: 1.25 picture pixels per world pixel and WebP under 120 KB (112 KB light, 116 KB dark); 2x was 152 KB and 201 KB at the lowest quality tried. One request, never an `<img>` or a preload, and never the LCP element (Chrome ignores a full-viewport image). Interleaved A/B on local production builds, iPhone 14, 4x CPU, Fast 4G, five pairs (light), medians: a full-looking map on screen 9,603 to 1,157 ms; the engine's map on screen 9,603 to 7,195 ms; `pubmax:pins-visible` 7,421 to 7,012 ms; LCP 3,464 to 3,400 ms (a text span in both); main-thread blocking before the map shows 5,214 to 3,314 ms. With no network throttle: 6,873 to 663, 6,873 to 5,021 and 5,448 to 4,442 ms. Dark, Fast 4G, three pairs: 8,114 to 1,630 ms for a full-looking map. Read honestly: `pubmax:map-style-load` was later in three of the five light Fast 4G pairs (median 4,407 to 5,111 ms) and earlier in the other two arms, so the picture's bytes may cost the engine some hundreds of milliseconds on a slow wire; the later marks did not move later. Part of the reveal's gain is the stand-down fix in the rule above (the old preview left at a resize and showed the animated loading scene again for three to four seconds), and the two are not separated here. Pins: `__tests__/mapOpeningSnapshot.test.ts`, `__tests__/mapOpeningPreviewDom.test.tsx`, `e2e/map-opening-preview.spec.ts` (the picture reaches past every screen edge while it shows, and the handoff never lands while the loading frame covers the canvas).

<a id="the-crawl-route-reads-like-directions-and-an-estimate-still-says-so"></a>

- **THE CRAWL ROUTE READS LIKE DIRECTIONS, AND AN ESTIMATE STILL SAYS SO.** By the captain's call (30 Oct 2026, "like Google Maps or Apple Maps"), the crawl route is drawn the way a maps app draws directions (`buildRoute` in `components/map/canvas/buildScene.ts`). FOUR rules. (1) THE LINE IS THICK AND SOLID ON ITS OWN EDGE: `--route-line` (`docs/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`) over a paper casing (ink in dark), 4px at zoom 12 to 9px at zoom 18, with the casing 3px wider each side at street level, so it reads as the way to go at a glance. (2) AN ESTIMATE STILL SAYS SO: a leg the router could not draw stays the straight line with the marching dash, and the map key keeps its "Dashed line" row. (3) EACH LEG CARRIES ITS TIME ON A BUBBLE: `buildRouteLegTimes` puts "6 min" on a paper pill (the `route-time` sprite image in `lib/mapIcons.ts`) at the middle of the drawn leg, from zoom 13.5 with the stop names. The minutes come from the leg's own length (`legMinutes` in `lib/routeLegs.ts`): the routed length and middle that `GET /api/walk-route` returns per leg (`legDistances` in `lib/walkRoute.ts`), or the straight length before that answer, printed "~6 min" because it is an estimate. A pin that collides with a bubble gives way, and the numbered stops take their space first. (4) THE CAMERA DOES NOT MOVE FOR IT: the line and the bubbles are drawn where the reader looks.
