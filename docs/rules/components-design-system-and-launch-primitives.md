<a id="coral-is-a-fill-and-coral-is-a-word-and-those-are-two-tokens"></a>

- **CORAL IS A FILL AND CORAL IS A WORD, AND THOSE ARE TWO TOKENS.** Captain's audit J37 section 5.5 asked whether the essential journeys work with a screen reader; the first measurement found ONE defect on six surfaces at once. `--brass` reads 2.49:1 to 2.91:1 as TEXT on the light elevation ladder, below AA at every step, while passing at 5.32:1 to 6.48:1 in dark, so every coral word on a light surface was failing and none of them had been fixed because nobody had measured the set. `--brass-ink` (`--color-accent-ink`) is the accent when it is a WORD: light-only, clearing 4.78:1 on the recessed `--panel`, 5.27:1 on `--paper` and 5.59:1 on a card; `app/theme.css` points it back at `--brass` so DARK KEEPS ONE CORAL. THREE rules. (1) It is deliberately NOT `--brick`: a price wears its band and no other colour, so a kicker in that crimson would say "expensive", and the ink is warmed to hue 12 against brick's 356. (2) A coral FILL is untouched and still carries `--color-on-accent`, and `--brass-accessible` stays the login primary alone; `.invitePreview__join` moved to `--color-on-accent` for the same reason a white label on coral was 3.41:1 in light and 2.15:1 over dark's amber. (3) OPACITY IS NOT A MUTED TOKEN: `opacity` on text composites the ink toward the surface where no token can see it, which is how an 11.5px caption went under AA; reach for `--color-text-muted`. THE SAME LAW HOLDS FOR EVERY HUE, not just coral: the plan surface printed its verdict pill and its crew stamp as their own hue on a 12 per cent tint of themselves (1.56:1 to 4.21:1 in light), so `--fit-ink` and `--plan-stamp-ink` in `app/plan/plan.css` deepen the hue toward `--ink` per theme, the derivation `app/theme.css` already uses for the price-band inks; and a TINTED panel lifts past the surface `--muted` was tuned against, so body copy on one takes `--color-text-soft`. THAT DERIVATION IS NOW A TOKEN ROW rather than a local answer each surface re-invents, because the 17 Sep 2026 sweep found the same shape on twenty-three more rules: `--tint-ink-accent`, `--tint-ink-positive`, `--tint-ink-caution`, `--tint-ink-negative` and `--tint-ink-info` (`app/globals.css`, retuned per theme in `app/theme.css`) are the hue as a WORD on a TINT of itself, one per semantic hue, and a badge reads one instead of writing a percentage. A LADDER INK IS NOT A TINT INK: `--brass-ink` is tuned against `--paper`, `--panel` and `--panel-raised`, and on a 12 per cent coral tint of the recessed panel it reads 4.25:1, which is how `/pubs` shipped 24 source badges at 3.75:1 and `/pint-index` its current-edition pill. THE ACCENT'S TINT INK IS DERIVED FROM `--brass-ink` AND NOT FROM RAW `--brass`: a straight mix of `--brass` and `--ink` lands on hue 357, which IS `--brick`'s hue, so the badges would have printed in the crimson the expensive price band owns, undoing the very reason `--brass-ink` is warmed to hue 12; the fence holds the two at least 10 degrees apart in light, and dark separates that pair by chroma instead. The other four tint inks DO sit close to their own band's ink, because they are the same hue deepened the same way, and what stops a badge reading as a price is the tint under it. Two traps the same sweep found and neither is visible to axe: an INLINE `outline: "none"` outranks every stylesheet rule and silently killed the global `:focus-visible` ring on the map's drink filter, and a sideways scrolling rail of cards holding no control of their own is unreachable by keyboard entirely unless the container is itself a tab stop. THE FENCES ARE THE MEASUREMENT: `e2e/a11y-core-journeys.spec.ts` holds axe serious/critical to 0 on nine core-journey routes at 390 and 1440 in light and dark under reduced motion, `e2e/a11y-keyboard-loop.spec.ts` drives discover, plan, share and contribute with no pointer at all, and `__tests__/accentInkContrast.test.ts` holds the shipped ratios on both ladders and IS A SWEEP rather than a file list: it walks every stylesheet under `app/**` and `components/**` except the two token owners themselves (`app/globals.css` and `app/theme.css`, covered by that file's own token assertions), resolves each rule's own `color`-over-`background` pair through `var()` and `color-mix()` in both themes AGAINST THE SURFACES THE DOM PAINTS (the `body` remap, never the `:root` values the map reads at documentElement), and refuses a hue on a tint of itself or a raw coral on a step of the ladder. TWO SHAPES IT CANNOT SEE and neither may be read as covered: a rule that declares no background of its own (the surface is an ancestor's decision) and a translucent fill (`color-mix(..., transparent)`, `rgba()`), both of which stay with the rendered fences. A pub is reachable without a pointer through the venue list (Layers then List view on desktop, More map controls on the phone), never the canvas. Measurement and before/after: `docs/proof/a11y-core-journeys/`.

<a id="next-image-drops-a-srcset-prop-and-a-proxied-photo-is-resized-through-its-loader"></a>

- **NEXT/IMAGE DROPS A `srcSet` PROP, AND A PROXIED PHOTO IS RESIZED THROUGH ITS LOADER.** `/pubs` shipped five photographs at their natural 1632x636 and 680x453 into a 344x168 box, 934 KB of it measured on a phone, and its load was the slowest on the site (Astra's live walk, 7 Sep 2026, finding B8). The bytes were never about the page: `/api/image-proxy` took no width and the cards emitted no `srcset`. FOUR rules. (1) THE SEAM IS THE `loader` PROP: next/image sets `srcSet` from its own attributes AFTER spreading the rest, so one passed in is silently dropped, and the first measured build shipped the natural bytes with the prop nowhere in the markup. (2) `VENUE_IMAGE_WIDTHS` (`lib/venueImages.ts`) is a CLOSED set and it is next/image's OWN candidate widths, so a srcset descriptor and the width the proxy really answers name the same number; a set of our own invention made the desktop card ask for 384 and be handed 688. The set is closed because the URL is the cache key, so an open range is a cache-buster and a CPU amplification vector on a route that fetches a remote image per request. (3) A SURFACE CAPS ITS OWN DENSITY through `maxWidth`, because a browser takes the LARGEST candidate when none is big enough: /pubs caps at 640 for a picture that sits under a gradient wash and a glyph shelf at 0.88 opacity, and the cost of that cap is named in the proof rather than hidden. (4) A COMMUNITY PHOTO IS NEVER RE-ENCODED: it is a signed Storage URL and keeps the `unoptimized` path exactly as it was, so only a `/api/image-proxy` URL is resizable. The proxy never enlarges, and a failed resize serves the original bytes, that failure being about us rather than about the picture. Pins: `__tests__/imageProxy.test.ts`; measurement `docs/proof/walk-bugs-one-liners/`.

<a id="every-first-run-question-gives-its-reason-and-only-the-ones-the-journey-uses"></a>

- **EVERY FIRST-RUN QUESTION GIVES ITS REASON, AND ONLY THE ONES THE JOURNEY USES.** Captain's "Ideal Flow" reference (24 Sep 2026, the Business of Apps deck): a reason lifted compliance from 60% to 94%, and a primed permission ask got 42% against 24%. `lib/onboardingFlow.ts` is the ONE place that names the first-run steps (London, budget, location, result, companion) and holds each question's copy beside the answer it explains, so a question cannot ship without its reason. FOUR rules. (1) A QUESTION IS ASKED ONLY WHEN THE JOURNEY USES THE ANSWER: the budget decides the count on the result screen, and a "usual order" question was left out because a card's listed price is the cheapest pint and no choice of drink would change it. (2) THE PRIVACY LINE SAYS ONLY WHAT THE CODE DOES: the budget stays on the device and the result reads it, and the location line is the near-me line ("We only use it to rank pubs nearby. Nothing is stored."), because the fix ranks the slim index in the browser and is never sent. (3) THE LOCATION ASK IS ITS OWN SCREEN, and nothing calls `getCurrentPosition` until its button is tapped; a refusal or a miss offers the eight night patches in the same words, never a dead end. (4) THE RESULT IS BUILT FROM THE ANSWERS, NEVER A TOUR: the cheapest listed pint and the next two come from `rankNearMe`, and the count of pubs under the budget is taken over EVERY priced pub inside the walk (`pricedWithinWalk`), not over the few cards the screen has room for, because "5 of these" over five cards read as a count of the area. The web reaches the journey only from the landing hero's primary, which carries `?start=web` for a first-time visitor (`webOnboardingStartRequested`, see THE FRONT DOOR above); a typed or shared URL still meets the edge 307. A web Skip lands where the hero was going (`/near?locate=1`), and a native Skip lands on Tonight. The web gate asks for the seen mark too, so a start-marked visit that already holds it (browser Back after finishing or skipping) goes on to `/near?locate=1` and never replays the journey. A venue read that could not run is its own answer with a retry, never an empty area, and a fix past the widened ring is outside coverage, never "near you". Pins: `__tests__/onboardingFlow.test.ts`, `__tests__/firstRunOnboarding.test.tsx`, `__tests__/landingPrimaryDoor.test.tsx`, `e2e/web-first-run-journey.spec.ts`, `e2e/mobile-first-run-onboarding.spec.ts`.
