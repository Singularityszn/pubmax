# Landing, city and listing pages

Moved from [`app/AGENTS.md`](../../app/AGENTS.md). Authoritative policy detail lives here; the area index stays short.

<a id="a-governed-price-landing-is-a-crawler-s-page-and-area-slug-is-held"></a>

- **A governed price landing is a crawler's page, and `/area/{slug}` is HELD.** Two families publish out of the bundled Pint Price dataset: `/drink/{brand}` and `/area/{area}/drink/{brand}`. The area landing page ITSELF is not published (captain decision 2026-08-15: it duplicates the linked `/borough/{slug}` family, and two crawlable families compete for one canonical), so the segment exists only as that page's parent, `/area/clapham` 404s, and NOTHING may link, breadcrumb or sitemap it. `lib/pricedLanding.ts` is the ONE seam both families share: the cheapest-first comparator, the publication floor TABLE, `publishablePricedRows` where `null` means the page does not exist so route, `generateStaticParams` and sitemap cannot disagree, the publisher and count sentences, the memoised `assignVenueToNightArea` and one JSON-LD builder, with `components/drinks/PricedLandingRows.tsx` as its rendering half. THREE rules. (1) The dataset is read ONLY through `lib/pintPriceLandingDataset.server.ts`, which delegates to `getPricedVenues()`; a loader of its own re-parses 6.7 MB per render, per metadata read, per OG card and per sitemap request. The `/borough` family reads it there too, after its own copy of that loader made it the slowest server render in `perf/route-budgets.json` (54 ms on CI, 117 ms on a laptop rig, against 10 ms once it shared the memo). `lib/boroughIndex.server.ts` then holds the two DERIVED tables the index prints, and `loadHistoricPubs` holds its own parse, both on the same rule: bundled build-time data, one read per process, and a read that FAILED is never cached, or one bad render leaves an empty city for the life of the process. That read has TWO answers: a page degrades to `[]` and 404s, the sitemap's `…OrThrow` wrapper fails loud, because a truncated sitemap deindexes what it dropped. (2) A landing CTA opens a PUB (`?sel=<venueId>`, plus `?brand=` alone), never `?q=<area name>`, which is a free-text venue filter an area name would narrow to nothing. It may name only a pub the map can RESOLVE, so `pricedLandingMapArrivalRow` takes the cheapest row inside the eager shard and `pricedLandingMapHref` drops `sel` entirely when there is none, over the TRI-STATE `lib/mapEagerVenueIndex.server.ts`; the ranked list never moves for a CTA, and `pricedLandingAreaMapCta` answers the href and the label together so a second predicate cannot re-decide it. (3) Publishing is NARROWER than route readiness: `nightAreaPublishesPrices` keeps the gate version and completeness predicates and drops only `isNightAreaRouteReady`'s review expiry, because a dated price list is not a route and expiring it would 404 indexed URLs. The brand page lists those published pair pages so they are not sitemap-only. Pins: `__tests__/nightAreaReviewRenewal.test.ts` (the renewal alarm, 30 days ahead), `__tests__/sitemap.test.ts`. Contract: `docs/specs/governed-priced-landings/{PRODUCT,TECH}.md`.

<a id="the-root-landing-lands-on-the-answer-and-the-first-tap-is-a-receipt"></a>

That swap reads `/api/venue/[id]`; the near-price-trust API withholds the page URL by contract. The archive travels as `LandingArchiveIndex`, built at prerender with its printed labels, so the browser never imports the history lane. The landing asks for location only on the card's quiet `Near me` control; an already-granted browser permission can trigger the swap without a prompt. The ranker and slim index load by dynamic `import()` only on that path. `buildLandingAnchorRail` supplies up to three next-cheapest pubs from the anchor's borough. The anchor is the captain's choice (`LANDING_PUB_PREFERENCE`), not re-derived from price, so the rail can undercut it. <a id="a-city-is-chosen-once-and-the-surfaces-that-read-it-follow"></a>

- **A city is CHOSEN once, and the surfaces that read it follow.** `/places` is the picker (`app/places/`, policy `lib/places.ts`, pure). Its one write is `writePreferredCity` (`lib/cityPreference.ts`), and Map (`MobileTabBar`, `SiteNav`), Create (`CreateFab`, `createFabActions`), Out (`app/out/OutClient.tsx` into `useOutListings`) and Near (`components/nearme/NearPageClient.tsx`) all read that ONE key. FOUR rules. (1) Map and Create resolve destinations through `preferredCityMapHref`; the [user-facing city navigation contract](../../README.md#features) owns link behaviour and the bookmark fallback. (2) Every gap is NAMED and the two gaps are separate findings with separate sentences: prices come from the `prices` profile in `lib/cityCapabilities.ts` and its own explanation is printed rather than re-worded, areas come from `getNightAreasForCity`, and a city with neither may not be worded as if it had one. Nothing invents a claim for a city we have not collected. (3) `/api/out` ANSWERS for every city the app knows (`OutCity` is `CityId`) and `isOutCityCovered` is the one coverage predicate, because a city a reader may CHOOSE must not make their own Out tab answer `INVALID_REQUEST`; the three cities outside the events refresh lane get the honest not-covered body instead. (4) The stored city is a browser value, so every reader is a `useSyncExternalStore` whose SERVER snapshot is null: `/` and `/map` are prerendered and CDN-held, and a city read at render time would put one stranger's choice into every stranger's document. `/places` IS the crawlable city list, and the only one: it carries the canonical (`alternates`) and the one sitemap row, and `/choose-city`, which has NO page, 308s to it at the edge (`proxy.ts`, paths in `lib/cityPickerRoute.ts`). Those three move in ONE commit, because a 308 from the page holding the canonical to a `noindex` page takes the city list out of the index rather than moving it. THE REDIRECT DROPS NO CAPABILITY: the retired address answered a TOWN, so the picker answers one too. With no city row matching a query of two characters or more, `placesTownResults` (`lib/places.ts`) falls back to `buildCityChooserSearchResults` over `public/data/uk_base/places.json`, read through `lib/useUkPlaceIndex.ts`. Three rules ride with it. (1) The fallback runs only on an EMPTY city list, because a query a city already answered would otherwise offer the same night twice. (2) An uncovered row prints the index's `context`, the postcode area, since the index holds 294 repeated names and two identical rows send a reader to a map hundreds of miles from the town they meant. (3) A mapless surface owes its own ODbL credit, so `/places` prints `components/city/PlaceIndexCredit.tsx` under the place names. Pins: `__tests__/placesTab.test.ts`, `__tests__/cityPickerRedirect.test.ts`, `__tests__/sitemap.test.ts`, `__tests__/navigationModel.test.ts`, `__tests__/mobileTabBar.test.ts`, `e2e/places-tab.spec.ts`, `e2e/places-town-fallback.spec.ts`.

<a id="the-answer-comes-first-and-what-acts-on-it-comes-after-it"></a>

- **THE ANSWER COMES FIRST, AND WHAT ACTS ON IT COMES AFTER IT.** `/tonight` put its head, the head's two way-onward doors, the freshness credits, the share control and nine vibe chips in front of the listings: measured on a production build with the acceptance spec's own mocked spine, the first row began at y=868 against a mobile tab bar at y=788 (390x844), and at y=972 against y=514 (320x568), so a phone met no listing. `Screen` carries `actionsAfterContent` (`components/ui/screen.tsx`, default false), which moves the way-onward row to the end of the screen. Tonight and the Places city picker use it so their answers precede the navigation actions. FOUR rules. (1) EVERY MOVE IS A DOM MOVE, NEVER A CSS `order`, so the reading order, the tab order and the paint order stay ONE order at every width; a phone-only `order` would hand a keyboard the doors before the thing they lead away from, which is the defect wearing a different hat. (2) ONE COMPOSITION AT EVERY WIDTH: the desktop grid places that row at the foot of the primary column rather than restoring it to the head, or the tab order disagrees with the paint again. (3) A REFINEMENT THAT IS THE LIST'S OWN CONTROL STAYS ABOVE THE LIST, and everything else follows it: the kind filter row in `app/tonight/tonight.css` leads the list and scrolls sideways on a phone rather than wrapping to a second 44px row, while the vibe chips are a mood ask and follow it, and the freshness stamp and Share sit under the listings they are about. (4) TAP TARGETS AND THE Day/Tonight PAIR ARE UNTOUCHED: a chip in a sideways rail keeps its 44px box and stays a tab stop, so the browser scrolls a focused chip into view. The first row then began at y=413 at 320x568, 360x640 and 390x844. Since #1627 the hyped pubs lead the lede region, so the pin holds the lede's first entry above the tab bar and the list inside that region. Pin: `e2e/tonight-trusted-ui.spec.ts` "keeps the main list before Deals/Music and above the mobile tab bar"; method, both tables and the shots are `docs/proof/tonight-first-row-fold/`.

<a id="the-homepage-share-card-is-the-invite-preview-so-it-may-hold-no-figure-of-its-ow"></a>

- **The homepage share card IS the invite preview, so it may hold no figure of its own.** `/r/<code>` 307s to `/#referral=<code>`, and a fragment never reaches the server, so what a crawler previews is the HOME document and its `og:image` is `/api/home-card`. No referral card exists or is needed. The card is drawn by `lib/homeOgCard.tsx` (route `app/api/home-card/route.tsx`, which carries the measurement for why it is a route), and its counts come from `loadAboutStats`, the SAME derived figures the landing hero and `/about` print: one claim, one source, and a figure typed in rots. Its hero and support line are the landing page's own h1 and lede, so a preview cannot promise what the page it opens does not say. Brand: `Wordmark` in `lib/ogBrand.tsx` is the site header's treatment re-expressed in px, never a boxed mark. Every mark on a card is cut from `MARK_POLYGONS` and held against `MARK_GEOMETRY` by the fence. Pin: `__tests__/homeOgCard.test.ts`.

<a id="the-privacy-notice-is-part-of-the-data-path-not-marketing-copy"></a>

- **The privacy notice is part of the data path, not marketing copy.** `/privacy` and `/terms` (`app/privacy`, `app/terms`, shared `app/legal.css`) describe exactly what the code does: consent-gated analytics, hashed-never-stored IPs, the named processors. Viewer points cross the one browser-and-server egress seam in `lib/geo.ts`; do not add caller-owned rounding, and keep public venue coordinates distinct from viewer coordinates. Durable `rate_limits` rows expire with their useful window and both database writers prune expired keys. Change a data practice and change those pages in the same commit. The one public contact address has its own entry below; never hardcode it into a page. A PROCESSOR IS NAMED FOR WHAT IT RECEIVES, AND A PROMPT IS NOT A VIEWER POINT: `__tests__/legalPages.test.ts` held only the location-recipient table, so the Arize AX tracing of #1693 sent a drinker's typed question to a US vendor the notice did not list and every fence stayed green. There are now three blocks instead of one - the viewer-point recipients, the PROMPT-TEXT recipients, and a SWEEP that reads every absolute endpoint under `lib/observability/` out of the code and fails on a host the page does not name, so the next model-observability vendor cannot land the same way. The page states the masking SCOPE the code applies (emails and `@handles`, the two patterns in `maskPersonalData`) and claims no more. Pins: `__tests__/legalPages.test.ts`, `__tests__/viewerCoordinateEgress.test.ts`, `__tests__/rateLimitExpiryMigration.test.ts`.

<a id="the-two-documents-a-reader-meets-on-their-worst-visit-are-governed-too"></a>

- **THE TWO DOCUMENTS A READER MEETS ON THEIR WORST VISIT ARE GOVERNED TOO.** `app/error.tsx` and `app/not-found.tsx` are styled INLINE, so no stylesheet sweep reaches them, and `__tests__/templatePatterns.test.ts` scopes itself to `components/`: between them the global boundary sat outside every fence in the repo and broke three laws at once (UI review 17 Sep 2026, finding 8). Its eyebrow was `textTransform: "uppercase"` at 0.14em, wider than the retired `--tracking-wider`, on plain text with no border and no fill, which the caps policy reserves for a STAMP alone; and it printed that eyebrow in `--brass`, 2.49:1 to 2.91:1 as text on the light ladder, the exact defect `--color-accent-ink` exists for. `__tests__/errorDocumentVoice.test.ts` is the fence and it is a CLASS rather than a file list: it walks `app/**` for every error, global-error and not-found document, refuses an uppercase transform, refuses tracking past 0.02em on plain text, refuses a raw coral word in every document, because none commits to a dark surface (the 404 follows the person's theme, `__tests__/notFoundPage.test.ts`), refuses a `var(--brass, #hex)` fallback naming the retired brass gold, and renders the boundary to hold it to the bare imperative `Try again` plus a way home. A text button in either document takes `--control-radius`, never `--radius-sm`.
